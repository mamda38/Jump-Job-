### Libraries offer more than books

Public libraries in several parts of the United States now offer more than books. Some run free fitness and cooking classes. Others provide food, conversations about mental health, or machines that check blood pressure. In Milwaukee, a mobile health team visits libraries and answers visitors' questions. Some visitors later decide to see a doctor.

These programs can reach people outside the usual health care system. Visitors do not need money, insurance, strong English skills, or identification to enter a library. This makes help easier to access for many residents.

Libraries are useful places for these services because people already see them as trusted sources of reliable information. They are also public spaces where people of different ages and backgrounds can meet. In small towns, a library may connect residents with services that are not available nearby.

However, a library cannot replace a hospital or a doctor. Its role is to offer basic support, share useful information, and help people find professional care when they need it.
